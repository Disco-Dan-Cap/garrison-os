A mood board that writes code

Maraschino is a design tool I built to improve my own AI workflow. You drop your inspiration images into the tool and add them to the dot-grid to make a mood board like an art director would do at the beginning of a project. Pin exactly what you like on your inspiration images, circle them, write a note, and rate each reference with cherries to add weight. Then you press Compile and watch the magic happen. The board turns into a brief and an extracted design file an AI coding tool can follow, along with generated image stills derived from everything you just pointed out.

Loading the model

The board is a very light slate blue with a dot grid. Every image sits in a polaroid frame: a thin white border and a deep chin. The frame gives it style and the chin is where the cherries live. Pan, zoom and select work exactly like Figma, because that is the muscle memory the people I built this for already have.

Seven pins on the rail

Every pin is a kind of claim: Color, Type, Format, Texture, Subject, Theme and Avoid. Each has its own hue and an icon that hints at the job, so the rail reads at a glance without labels. Hover a disc and its name pops out with a little bounce; the keys 1 to 7 arm the next pin from the keyboard. The set is not fixed. If a new kind of claim earns a place it gets a pin, and there is room in the hues to keep them apart: lighter and darker versions, or a neon, for the next ones.

Pin, circle, say why.

Hover a pin and three bubbles split out of it: note, draw around it, delete. The lasso is a freehand loop in the pin's own hue, and its boundary is the crop the model gets. The note rides along with the crop. For example, in this image the model sees the character and the pose you circled, and it sees what you wrote about it and why you like it, instead of just ingesting fifty whole images.

One image, several opinions.

The same photo can hold a Subject pin for the pose and a Color pin for the backdrop. Each becomes its own crop, its own note, its own line in the extraction. A black Avoid pin does the opposite job: never this.

Weight is per image, zero to three cherries in the chin, labeled noted, important, non-negotiable. The cherries are the quantity and the words are the judgement.

The tray

Images arrive before they are placed.

Imports land in a tray across the top, a holding shelf you drag from one image at a time. Thumbnails magnify under the pointer with distance falloff so you view them easier. Run your pointer across this one and watch it work.

Boards

Everything stays on this computer.

Boards, images, pins, briefs and stills live in the browser's IndexedDB. There is no account and no upload step. One board is on the table; the rest wait on a shelf with their camera position remembered.

Why it exists

I keep a cork board at home covered in torn-out ads, film stills, and product photos, with pins on the exact things I like. When I tried to hand that same board to AI as fifty images and a paragraph, it blended everything into one generic vibe. Nothing told it that I wanted the grain from one photo, the layout from another, and to ignore the rest. Maraschino is that cork board as software. You pin what you specifically like about each image, weight what matters most, and it compiles a design file and a brief the AI can actually follow.

To validate the idea, I ran the same fifty images through both methods, same image generator producing several outputs, and built a quick scoring page that shuffled the results so I could vote blindly on which ones I liked best. The paragraph version spit out collages of the mood board itself, with fake logos I'd specifically said to avoid. The Maraschino version won every time. The images looked like the brand, not like a pile of references.

Design decisions

  • Glossy pins on the board, flat discs in the rail. A small glass ball-head keeps the seven hues apart down to 20px. In the toolbar the icon does the identifying, so the discs stay flat.
  • A cool board so warm references pop. Reference imagery runs warm, so the surface is pale slate blue with a dot grid and the warmth lives in the cherry red, the pins and the images.
  • One weight per image, as cherries in the chin. Zero to three: noted, important, non-negotiable. Click the lit cherry to clear it. Try it below.
  • Figma's muscle memory, not its look. V and H, space to hand, zoom on the cursor, shift-1 to fit, command-Z. Placing a pin is one-shot and 1 to 7 rearms the rail.
  • About 5% skeuomorphism. No paper grain on the canvas. The objects carry the physicality: springy motion and real shadows under one shared light.
