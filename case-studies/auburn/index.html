SHIFT

Dry-powder inhaler
SponsorCSP TechnologiesYear2017

SHIFT is a dry-powder inhaler (DPI) I developed with CSP Technologies. The brief was a single injection-moldable case for the medication: airtight, with a distinctive opening action.

This project taught me how many constraints come with injection-molding plastic products. Rather than letting the production process limit the form, I learned to design with the mold and push what it could do.

VERVE

Smart-home ceiling fan
SponsorEmerson TechnologiesYear2017

VERVE is a smart-home ceiling fan I developed with Emerson Technologies. The goal was a fan that works in any home, old or modern. A ceiling fan sits high and dead-center in the room, which makes it a natural hub for smart-home tech.

Verve has three foam blades, a child-monitor and security camera, and a Bluetooth speaker ring. The foam blades use a Nerf-inspired material, which I landed on after my research turned up a pattern of kids hurting their hands or hitting their heads on fan blades while jumping on their beds.

Amber

Flat-pack paper luminaire
WhereNCAD, DublinFormatThree-day workshop

Amber is a flat-pack paper luminaire I designed in a three-day workshop at the National College of Art & Design in Dublin, during my study-abroad semester.

My first sketches were organic and kinetic. As I kept sketching I realized I could get the same sense of movement from several simpler, repeated pieces. Quarter-scale models showed how the paper would take the shape, and full-scale models settled the connection points and the hanging. In the final piece, string tightens the paper into shape: the bottom piece hangs from the wire and every other piece simply stacks on top of it.

Process

Sketch, model, light.

Role
Concept sketching, quarter- and full-scale paper models, and the final luminaire.

05

HIGHdrate

Hydration harness for workers at height
SponsorWestern ForgeTypeResearch → full-scale model

HIGHdrate is a hydration harness for construction workers, a studio project for Western Forge. The brief started broad: observe construction sites and design tools that improve the daily lives of the people working them.

Field research with ironworkers and telephone-tower inspectors narrowed it down. Everyone agreed staying hydrated is crucial to working safely, but drinking from a bottle while climbing a 300-foot tower is tricky and dangerous, and the one product on the market sat on the upper back, loaded the shoulders, and meant taking off the whole harness to refill it.

HIGHdrate moves the water to the lower lumbar so the weight rides on the hips. Dual zippers open the pouch from either side, so it can be pulled around to the front, refilled and put back without taking the harness off, and the hose runs up the back through a protective channel. The pouch is Camelbak’s Antidote Lumbar Reservoir.

Process

Problem to final model.

Role
Field research, concept sketching, full-scale model construction, and the final model.
Insight
Put the weight on the hips, and make the pouch refillable without unclipping from the harness.
